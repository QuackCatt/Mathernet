# Formula introduction skill

Read these instructions before generating a formula introduction.

## Purpose

Write a compact, reliable introduction to one mathematical formula for an independent learner. The introduction should explain what the formula expresses, what mathematical objects or quantities it relates, where it is typically used, and why it matters.

## Writing rules

- Produce one coherent paragraph of **110-150 words**.
- Begin with a direct definition rather than a generic opening.
- Explain the formula's meaning and practical role without turning the passage into a derivation.
- Use clear, calm academic English and concrete mathematical language.
- Write mathematical expressions as LaTeX between single dollar delimiters, such as `$e^{i\\pi}+1=0$`.
- Do not include a title, headings, bullet lists, citations, source links, or placeholders.
- Do not invent formulas, notation conventions, historical claims, people, quotations, applications, or sources.
- Do not solve exams, assessed work, or homework.

## Research rules

- Use the model's built-in web search when available to verify the definition and common uses.
- When supplied source material conflicts with general knowledge, treat the supplied material as the factual boundary.
- If research is unavailable, keep the introduction to established mathematical meaning and avoid uncertain historical or application claims.
